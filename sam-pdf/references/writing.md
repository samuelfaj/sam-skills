# Writing the copy

- Write in the language the user asked for, for the stated audience. People who have never seen the product need the what and the why of each screen, not field-by-field narration.
- Each page: a short title that names the step, then 1-3 short sentences. Say who acts, what they see, and what happens next.
- Every claim comes from the source: code, configuration, the captured screen, or the user. Check behavior claims (timers, limits, who is notified, what is stored) in the code before writing them. If you cannot confirm a claim, cut it.
- Never promise outcomes, features or timings the product does not have. Follow the project's own copy and compliance rules when it has them.
- Use the product's own names for screens and buttons, quoted exactly as they appear. Define a term the first time; then keep using the same term.
- Disclose the setup on an early page: test environment, fictional people and payment data, placeholder images, and the capture date.
- End with an open-items page: defects and rough edges found while building the document, and anything not verified. Plain and specific, no blame.
- When a humanizer skill is installed (for example `sam-humanizer`), run the final copy through it in the target language, then re-check that no fact changed.
- No emoji, no filler, no exclamation marks, no marketing superlatives.
